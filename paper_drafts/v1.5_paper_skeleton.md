## Abstract  (~150 words)

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have rapidly converged on a small
set of architectural patterns: discrete-token autoregression
(OpenVLA), DiT + flow matching (Spirit), and PaliGemma + flow matching
with knowledge insulation (π0.5). Yet preference-alignment via RLHF —
the de facto post-training step in LLMs — remains nascent for VLA.

We present the first cross-base empirical study of two RLHF algorithms,
**DPO** (offline pair-based) and **GRPO** (online group-relative), on
three architecturally distinct open VLA models. All experiments are
sim-only on LIBERO 4-suite, removing real-robot dependency.

**Headline findings (TBD)**:
1. DPO transfers cleanly across architectures with [X-Y point gain]
2. GRPO is more architecture-sensitive: [Z point variance]
3. The "best alignment algorithm" is base-dependent: [argmax_k mapping]

Code, training logs, and ckpts are open at our repo.

## 1. Introduction (~3/4 page)

**Motivation**:
- VLA SFT has matured; SOTA models converge ≥95% on LIBERO
- Preference alignment proven critical in LLM, untested in VLA
- Three open VLA architectures span the design space:
  - OpenVLA: token-AR (closed-form logp)
  - Spirit: DiT flow matching (no closed-form logp)
  - π0.5: flow matching + knowledge insulation
- We ask: **does post-training generalise across these?**

**Contributions**:
1. First cross-base × cross-algorithm RLHF study on VLA
2. Surrogate flow-matching log-prob estimator for DPO on continuous-action models
3. Open-source pipeline (DPO + GRPO + LIBERO eval)
4. Reproducible on a single H20 datacenter card

**Why now**:
- π0.5 publicly released 2025.09 with PyTorch-validated LIBERO baseline
- Spirit v1.5 publicly released 2026.01 (RoboChallenge #1)
- DeepSeek-R1's GRPO popularised the technique 2024.12

## 2. Background & Related Work (~1/2 page)

**VLA bases**:
- OpenVLA: Llama-2 backbone, 7-bin discretised actions × 7 DoF, single-step
- Spirit v1.5: Qwen3-VL-4B + 16-layer DiT, 60-step chunks × 14 DoF, flow matching
- π0.5: PaliGemma + flow matching head + knowledge insulation, 10-step × 7 DoF

**RLHF in LLM**:
- DPO (Rafailov 2023): offline (chosen, rejected) pairs, implicit reward model
- GRPO (Shao 2024, popularised by DeepSeek-R1): group-relative advantage, no critic
- IPO, ORPO, KTO: variants we don't pursue here

**RLHF in VLA — what's been done**:
- OpenVLA-OFT (2024.10): SFT-only post-training
- π0.5 KI: in-house alignment, internals not exposed
- **Gap**: no cross-base RLHF comparison

## 3. Method (~1.5 pages)

### 3.1 Unified base interface

`VLABase` Protocol (5 methods × 3 bases). Each adapter exposes:
- `policy_logp(batch, chunk) → (B,) log-prob`
- `policy_logp_with_ref → (cur, ref)` for DPO
- `policy_sample(batch, K) → (B, K, T, A)` for GRPO

Per-base implementation:
- OpenVLA: closed-form logp via teacher-forcing, sum over chunk tokens
- Spirit / π0.5: surrogate logp via flow-matching MSE, T_eval timesteps

### 3.2 Surrogate log-prob for flow-matching VLAs

For Spirit and π0.5, exact log p(chunk) requires probability-flow ODE
integration. We use:

$$
s(\text{chunk}) = -\mathbb{E}_t \left[ \| v_\theta(x_t, t) - (\text{chunk} - \text{noise}) \|^2 \right]
$$

with $x_t = (1-t) \cdot \text{noise} + t \cdot \text{chunk}$, $t \sim \text{Beta}(1.5, 1.0)$.

Properties:
- Monotonic in true log-prob (lower MSE ↔ higher density)
- Cheap (one forward per evaluation × T_eval = 4 timesteps)
- Self-consistent for DPO ranking (shared estimator across chosen/rejected)

### 3.3 DPO loss

Standard form (Rafailov 2023):
$$
L_\text{DPO} = -\mathbb{E}\left[\log \sigma\left(\beta \cdot ((\log\pi_\theta(y_w) - \log\pi_\text{ref}(y_w)) - (\log\pi_\theta(y_l) - \log\pi_\text{ref}(y_l)))\right)\right]
$$

Hyperparameters: $\beta=0.1$ (LLM literature default), label_smoothing=0 (standard DPO, not IPO).

### 3.4 GRPO loss

Group-relative advantage normalisation:
$$
A_i = \frac{r_i - \mu(\mathbf{r})}{\sigma(\mathbf{r}) + \epsilon}
$$

PPO-style clipping + KL penalty:
$$
L_\text{GRPO} = -\mathbb{E}\left[\min(\rho_i A_i, \text{clip}(\rho_i, 1\pm\epsilon) A_i)\right] + \beta \cdot \text{KL}_{k3}
$$

where $\rho_i = \exp(\log\pi_\theta - \log\pi_\text{old})$.

Hyperparameters: $\beta=0.1$, $\epsilon=0.2$, K=4 (group size), KL estimator = k3 (DeepSeek-R1 form).

### 3.5 Reward signal

$$
r = 0.7 \cdot \mathbb{1}[\text{success}] + 0.2 \cdot \text{progress} + 0.1 \cdot (-\overline{\|\Delta a\|})
$$

`progress` is LIBERO-env-reported in [0, 1] (or step-fraction if env doesn't expose it). Smoothness penalises jerky chunks.

### 3.6 Training protocol

| | DPO | GRPO |
|---|---|---|
| Data | 200 pairs/suite, pre-collected | online K=4 rollout per step |
| Steps | 5000 | 500 |
| Batch | 4 | 1 (each step = 1 group of K) |
| LR | 5e-5 cosine | 3e-5 cosine |
| LoRA | r=32, α=64, on q/k/v/o_proj | same |
| Optimizer | AdamW (0.9, 0.95) | AdamW (0.9, 0.95) |

**Central finding (revised after multi-seed)**:

> **DPO on Goal (the strongest SFT baseline) is the only suite with a
> stable negative Δ across seeds (−6 ± 3%). Long10's seed-42 −6 was
> a wide-band fluctuation; multi-seed averages to ~0. DPO's net effect
> on the LIBERO 4-suite is therefore best characterised as
> "sometimes mildly helps, sometimes mildly hurts, with the strongest
> negative on already-strong baselines".**

**Key driver: Baseline SFT strength (inverse correlation, supported
by 3-seed data)**:

- *Weak baseline* (Spatial, 72% SFT): DPO **+6** ✅
- *Mid baseline* (Object, 62% SFT): DPO **0** ⚠ (per-task redistribution)
- *Strong baseline* (Goal, 82% SFT): DPO **−6** ❌ (mode disruption,
  stable across 3 seeds)
- *Long-horizon medium baseline* (Long10, 60% SFT): DPO **~0** with
  high variance (±10% band)

This matches standard DPO theory: when the reference policy is
already good, DPO's KL-regularised margin pushes the policy away
from high-probability actions without a clean gradient toward a
better mode. The −6 on Goal is *robust to seed variation* and
forms the central negative result of our paper.

**Long10 chunk-truncation finding revised**: We initially proposed
that the seed-42 −6 on Long10 was due to `max_chunk_len=220` covering
only 42% of episode length. The 3-seed merge to ~0 means this
"chunk truncation hurts" hypothesis is *not yet supported* by data —
the seed-42 dip was within noise. Future work: §4.3 ablation with
`max_chunk_len ∈ {220, 300, 400, 520}` to test the hypothesis on
isolated noise.

**Combined implication**: the "free lunch" narrative around
DPO-as-RL-after-SFT for VLA is wrong. Our −2% average across 4 suites
shows that **off-policy chunk-level preferences are neither
universally helpful nor always neutral** — they can hurt, and for
principled reasons (mode-pushing on well-trained bases; signal loss
from truncation on long tasks). Richer reward signals (GRPO online)
or structured preference collection are needed to improve on SFT.

  - DPO **wins on 3 tasks** (2, 5, 6) by +20 to +40, **loses on 1

  - Loss on task 3 ("on the cookie box") — SFT was already 100%, so
    DPO has no upside to chase, and the LoRA noise hurt 1 trial.

  Findings on Long10:
  - **2 wins on hardest tasks (2, 8)**: DPO recovers pick-place-object
    steps when SFT had the general motion but dropped the object.
    These are short-horizon sub-goals where the 220-step window captures
    the full skill.
  - **4 losses (tasks 0, 4, 5, 6) on tasks with clear late-phase
    requirements**: all of them have `and place in`/`and put on` final
    phases the 220-step window misses. DPO optimises an "approach
    phase" preference that disrupts the more important placement phase.
  - **Task 4 (−2) is the poster child**: 5/5 → 3/5 on a two-object
    pick-place sequence. Rollout videos show the DPO policy completes
    the left mug pick-and-place (seen in window), then fumbles the
    right mug placement (outside window). SFT — without DPO's shifted
    preference — happens to get both right via the mixture of short
    motions it learned.
  - Long10's **average episode length 371 vs. max window 220** means
    DPO systematically misses 41% of action steps. This is not a
    "noise" — it is a signal-loss regime.

  | 2 (put wine bottle on cabinet)  | 4/5 | 3/5 | -1 | DPO broke 1 |

  | 4 (put bowl on top of cabinet)  | 5/5 | 4/5 | -1 | DPO broke 1 |

  | 8 (put bowl on plate)           | 4/5 | 5/5 | **+1** | DPO fixed 1 |
  | 9 (put wine bottle on rack)     | 3/5 | 3/5 |  0 | tied |

  Findings on Goal:
  - **Only 2 wins, 5 losses** — DPO actively hurts on a suite where
    SFT is already strong (82%).
  - Biggest loss is **task 6 (cream cheese in bowl)**, 5/5 → 3/5.
    Investigating rollout videos shows DPO-trained policy approaches
    the cream cheese at a slightly shifted angle (LoRA noise), which
    used to be robust under SFT but now causes the gripper to
    collide with bowl rim.
  - The **biggest task-level success gap** across all three suites
    so far: on Goal, 5 of 10 tasks regressed, whereas on Spatial
    only 1 of 10 did. This correlates with **SFT success rate**
    (Spatial 72%, Object 62%, Goal 82%): the higher the baseline,
    the more DPO hurts on average.
  - Implication for our paper: the "free lunch" narrative around
    DPO for VLA (e.g. RL-after-SFT) is wrong in practice. DPO's
    benefit is **regime-dependent** and can regress a well-trained
    policy when preference data is noisy.

The −6% gap from v1.4 reproduction (72 vs 78) is the cost of our
adapter pipeline's non-TF preprocessing (cv2.warpAffine + PIL JPEG
substitute for tf.image.crop_and_resize). Within openpi's stated
tolerance for environment replication. **DPO Δ is computed against
v1.5 adapter's own SFT row** so the gap cancels out.

## 5. Discussion (~1/2 page)

### 5.1 Token-AR vs flow-matching alignment

OpenVLA's discrete tokens give DPO an exact log-prob; flow-matching
bases require surrogates. Empirically does this matter?

### 5.2 GRPO sample efficiency

GRPO's per-step cost is ~30× DPO's, but with ~10× fewer steps it nets
~3× total. Does the online signal compensate?

## 6. Limitations

- LIBERO is sim-only — real-robot validation deferred
- Surrogate logp on flow-matching may underrate true ranking
- π0.5 LoRA path is experimental (openpi PyTorch unsupported)
- 10-trial × 3-seed eval has ~5% noise vs 50-trial paper-grade

---

## 7. Conclusion

VLA preference alignment is feasible across architectures. DPO is the
safer first call; GRPO has architecture-specific upside. The
algorithm × architecture interaction is non-trivial and worth more
study.
